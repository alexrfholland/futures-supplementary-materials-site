Appendix A: Glossary

The main text reframes several concepts to support our approach to nonhuman leadership. We provide a brief glossary of these terms here.

Processes

  • Community: In more-than-human terms (combining community ecology, political studies, etc), ‘community’ are practised relationships at a location, which create fuzzy, emergent groups consisting of humans as well as non-humans, refer 1.

  • Path: A depiction of a process that show changes to a community over time.

  • Possibility space: The search space that describes all possible paths.

  • Resistance: Multiple interacting constraints that make a path through the possibility space more or less likely. This includes functional, developmental, historical, genetic, political constraints and rigidity traps (ie. regulatory frameworks, time, and institutional capacity that lock managers into practices that appear safe but prevent adaptation). We implement resistance as a score from 0 to 100, where 0 indicates no resistance to change.

  • Destination: A potential (ie. probable, plausible, implausible, preferable) region of the possibility space.

  • Trend: The most likely path, ie. the path of least resistance.

  • Transformation: Fundamental shift away from the trend.

  • Pathway: An intentionally manipulated path created through a series of interconnected interventions aimed at reaching better destinations.

States

  • State: An observable instance along a path at a point in time.

  • Habitat volume: A way of representing a state as a three-dimensional grid of 25 × 25 × 25 cm cells, or “voxels”, each containing multiple attributes, an extension of prior work2. Habitat volumes are macroscale urban spatial units that include neighbourhoods or districts. Attributes express dynamic relationships defined by the participatory pipeline that include perching, sheltering and ageing in place. Our model assigns indicators to all habitat volumes deemed potentially “liveable” for the selected personas.

  • Habitat features: A way of differentiating the habitat volume. These aggregate habitat opportunities and represent vegetation, soil, water or artificial structures. Our current implementation focuses on trees.

  • Canopy footprint and patch resistance: The model aggregates resistance values to calculate “canopy footprint resistance” beneath habitat features and a “patch resistance” across building and ground surfaces. These metrics assess the effort required to create connections between features and support nonhuman proposals.

Leadership

  • Design: The collective creation of conditions that support life in response to novelty.

  • Thriving: The measure we use to evaluate preferability along a path.

  • Intervention: Interventions are actions or strategies implemented to protect, restore, enhance, or manage biodiversity. They might include habitat restoration, species reintroduction, grazing management, or creation of artificial structures (e.g., nest boxes, deadwood habitats, green roofs) 3 as well as wildlife-friendly building standards, and biodiversity-inclusive approaches to urban planning, interventions into human behaviours or cultures such as through education or incentives.

  • Proposal: A way to sampling the possibility space. A possible way of living by individual or groups of community members. Proposals are the mechanisms where nonhuman beings identify ways of changing the habitat volume, either as individual organisms (as in the case of Elm personas proposing to complete full lifecycles) or as aggregated groups (Bird and Lizard personas proposing interventions such as depaving or deploying artificial trees

  • Effort: The degree of freedom interventions have to overcome resistance. We implement effort as thresholds (0-100) per intervention type, which determine a community’s capacity to consider and implement proposals. At each location, if resistance is less than or equal to the effort threshold, the community enables proposals through compatible interventions; if resistance exceeds the threshold, the community constrains proposals. Changes in effort thresholds over time reflect shifts in technology, understanding, costs, labour, or community perceptions. We vary effort at discrete points in time to define the conditions of a pathway. The model then calculates actualised interventions and the resulting pressure put on the path.

  • Leadership: An increase in the probability of thriving. We implement “leadership” as the likelihood that nonhuman proposals influence or redirect a pathway.

Inclusion

  • Place: A way to sample a community. Places are assemblages of abiotic and biotic interactions at a location.

  • Persona: A fictional representation of a community member based on quantitative data. We select “birds”, “lizards” and “trees” as urban taxa that persist at the case places and qualify as “ambassadors”. Our innovation is to treat nonhuman personas not as users but as co-designers, referred to as “designer personas”.

  • Capabilities: A way to measure of thriving. Capabilities are conditions that enable a persona to lead a good life.

  • Relational indicators: A way to track capabilities using measurable “indicators” that reflect liveable conditions for personas. We draw on ecological assessments of woodlands to define indicators as numerical expressions of relational capabilities. For example, the volume of dead branches may indicate birds’ capacity for perching and social interaction.

  • Experiences: Pathway states as encountered by personas. They concretise what it would be like for different community members (birds, lizards, trees, humans) to live in the modelled futures.

Appendix H: Baseline Woodlands

This appendix documents the baseline categories small, medium, large, senescing, snag, fallen, and decayed for a pre-colonial south-eastern Australian eucalypt woodland baseline.

Overview

The model classes are defined as:

model category baseline interpretation main data source
small, medium, large live trees, grouped by DBH class Gibbons et al. Flat stem-density predictions1
senescing live large trees in declining crown condition Killey et al. crown-condition scenarios2
snag additional standing dead stems Killey et al. dead-stem estimates3
fallen fallen trunks / large fallen tree sections calculated from total deadwood minus smaller fallen branch debris, using Manning et al. and Killey et al.45
decayed smaller fallen branch fragments and advanced-decay ground debris Killey et al. fallen branch debris estimates, scaled within the total deadwood target6

The total ground-deadwood target is set from Mac Nally et al.’s unmanaged Millewa reference and converted from mass to volume using 0.6 t/m3.7

Small, Medium and Large

Our estimate for small, medium, and large comes from the Flat predictions in Gibbons et al. for stands under relatively little modification by humans since European settlement, not from a strict direct measurement of pre-European conditions.8

The Flat values are treated as densities per 0.1 ha and scaled to the site area. The values used for the baseline are:

DBH row Flat density per 0.1 ha density per ha
5 cm 6.2 62.0
21 cm 3.4 34.0
31 cm 2.6 26.0
41 cm 0.6 6.0
51 cm 0.7 7.0
61 cm 0.365 3.65
71 cm 0.56 5.6
80 cm 1.02 10.2

These DBH rows are grouped into the model size classes as follows:

model category model definition DBH rows included density per 0.1 ha density per ha
small DBH < 50 cm 5, 21, 31, 41 12.8 128.0
medium DBH >= 50 cm and < 80 cm 51, 61, 71 1.625 16.25
large DBH >= 80 cm 80 1.02 10.2

In the model, these live trees are treated as pre-colonial reserve trees and represented with pre-colonial tree templates for the corresponding size class.

Senescing and Snag

For senescing and snag, we use Killey et al. Table 7. This lets us define senescing as a portion of the live large-tree class, and snag as additional standing dead stems.9

Killey includes estmiates for a >60 cmtrunk size tree as 15 live stems/ha. These >60 cm stems are split into crown-condition classes:

  • 11-12 of 15 live stems/ha are below 70% crown, which we treat as the broad senescing reference range.
  • 4-6 of 15 live stems/ha are below 30% crown, which we treat as the severe senescing reference range.
  • 3-6 dead stems/ha are present as standing dead trees, which we use as the snag reference range.

We define senescing as 65% of the live large cohort. This is a modelling value within the Killey-derived condition range below the broad 73-80% share of live >60 cm stems below 70% crown in Scenarios 1-2, but above the narrower 27-40% share below 30% crown. We do this because our large class is stricter than Killey et al.’s >60 cm class and so we use a conservative partition of the current live large cohort rather than adopting Killey’s 15 live stems/ha directly.

Using the live large density of 10.2 stems/ha, this gives:

class relation to live large cohort stems/ha before site-level rounding
retained healthy large 35% of live large 3.57
senescing 65% of live large 6.63

snag is defined as additional standing dead stems equal to 30% of the live large cohort. This is the midpoint of Killey et al.’s estimates for their Scenario 1-2 as additional trees. This comes out to 3-6 dead stems per 15 live >60 cm stems, or 20-40% of the live large-stem cohort.

Using the live large density of 10.2 stems/ha, this gives 3.06 snags/ha

Fallen and Decayed

We calculate fallen and decayed by refering to the literature on estimating ground deadwood biovolume, portioning this volume across these two classes, and then adding fallen and decayed features to the volume until these volumes are reached.

The evidence base for the ground-deadwood target and split is:

measure quantity type of estimate ref note
total coarse woody debris 125 t/ha, approximately 208.3 m3/ha using 0.6 t/m3 baseline estimate Mac Nally et al. 200210 unmanaged Millewa reference
fallen branch debris 7.0-11.9 m3/ha baseline estimate Killey et al. 201011 yellow box-red gum woodland fallen branch debris
total coarse woody debris 19.3 m3/ha median, 34.0 m3/ha mean, range 3.0-247.2 m3/ha condition estimate Manning et al. 200712 box-gum grassy woodland reserves
forest-floor coarse woody debris 18.9 t/ha in woodland, 50.4 t/ha in open forest condition estimate Woldendorp and Keenan 200513 broader Australian woodland/open-forest context
total coarse woody debris 114-166 m3/ha baseline estimate Threlfall et al. 201914 eastern Australian native forests

For the total ground-deadwood target, we use Mac Nally et al.’s unmanaged Millewa reference of 125 t/ha.15 This is converted to volume with the model wood density of 0.6 t/m3:

volume (m3/ha) = mass (t/ha) / 0.6 (t/m3)
125 t/ha / 0.6 t/m3 = 208.3 m3/ha

For the split between fallen and decayed, we use two quantities:

  • Manning et al. provide total fallen deadwood volumes for box-gum grassy woodland reserves: 19.3 m3/ha median and 34.0 m3/ha mean.16
  • Killey et al. provide smaller fallen branch debris volumes, excluding fallen tree trunks or boles: 7.0-11.9 m3/ha.17

We then calculate the fallen trunk component as:

fallen trunks = total fallen deadwood - smaller fallen branch debris

This gives:

case total fallen deadwood smaller fallen branch debris calculated fallen trunks fallen trunk share smaller debris share
lower woodland case 19.3 m3/ha 9.5 m3/ha 9.8 m3/ha 51% 49%
upper woodland case 34.0 m3/ha 11.9 m3/ha 22.1 m3/ha 65% 35%

The baseline adopts a trunk-weighted 70% / 30% split. This slightly emphasises fallen trunks while still retaining a substantial smaller-debris class:

model category share of total ground deadwood target volume from 208.3 m3/ha
fallen 70% 145.81 m3/ha
decayed 30% 62.49 m3/ha

The model has explicit object volumes for tree, bole, and debris templates. It therefore adds fallen and decayed template objects progressively until each target volume is met or slightly exceeded.

The accumulation logic is:

  1. Set the total ground-deadwood volume target.
  2. Allocate 70% of that target to fallen.
  3. Allocate 30% of that target to decayed.
  4. Add template objects until the fallen and decayed targets are reached.
